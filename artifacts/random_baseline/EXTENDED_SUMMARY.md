## Caveats

### Multiplication label imbalance
For modular multiplication (a·b mod 97), the label distribution is not uniform:
label 0 appears **193×** (all pairs where a=0 or b=0) vs 96× for labels 1–96.
This raises the chance baseline from 1/97 ≈ 0.0103 to **~0.019** (always predict 0).
The onehot and random controls achieving ~0.015–0.019 on multiplication is entirely
explained by this imbalance — they learn "if a=0 or b=0, predict 0" and guess uniformly
otherwise. This is not evidence of arithmetic structure.

### Random controls pool 25 draws (5 main seeds × 5 sub-seeds)
The 5 random sub-seeds (random-0 through random-4) are independent random network
initializations nested under each of the 5 main seeds. Pooling all 25 into one
mean±std gives the best-estimated population statistic for an untrained random network.
Std is computed over the full 25-element array (`np.std(all_25)`), not averaged.

### MLP logit lens numbers are under the layernorm-inclusive objective
Unlike the pre-layernorm-fix baseline (where MLP achieved 0.5066), these numbers
reflect training through `final_layernorm`. The MLP's partial success (0.70 add, 0.45 mult)
shows that nonlinearity + bias helps but still falls far short of the grokked model's 1.0.
The α-sweep behavior of the MLP control is not computable from this data — all alpha
columns are 0.0000 because `--logit-lens-only` mode skips the Phi-2 forward pass.

### Logit lens vs probe gap
The probe consistently shows lower accuracy than the logit lens for the MLP condition.
This is expected: the probe trains a separate LogisticRegression on the raw 2560-dim
hidden state, while the logit lens measures the lm_head's ability to decode the same
hidden state after a layernorm transform. The gap reflects the different inductive biases
of the two decoders, not a measurement error.
